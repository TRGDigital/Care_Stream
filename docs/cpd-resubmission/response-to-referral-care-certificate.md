## Observations

## Areas needing improvement

**2. Certificate title.** The module, its certificate, the navigation guide and
the submission form now all carry the single title "Care Certificate Annual
Training". The certificate prints the module title directly from the course
record, so the two cannot diverge again.

**3a. 16 standards.** The course has been rebuilt in full against the March 2025
Care Certificate standards. It now contains sixteen sections, one per standard,
using the current standard titles, including standard 4 (Equality, diversity,
inclusion and human rights), the narrowed standard 9 (Awareness of mental health
and dementia), standard 10 (Adult safeguarding) and the new standard 16
(Awareness of learning disability and autism). Content was written against the
published assessment outcomes in the Skills for Care standards document and the
assessor and employer guide (March 2025), both of which are cited in the course
references.

**3b. Course structure.** The six broad modules the assessor reviewed have been
replaced by the sixteen standard-aligned sections described above. The
navigation guide has been rewritten to describe this structure, and now matches
the online content exactly: 16 sections, each comprising a lesson, a workplace
scenario and an in-lesson knowledge check.

**3c. Interactive activities.** Each of the sixteen sections now carries three
distinct interactive elements: a workplace case study (a situation, a prompt for
the learner's own decision, then modelled best practice), a hands-on activity,
and a knowledge check with immediate feedback. The activities are tap-and-drag
exercises of three kinds, chosen to suit the standard: putting the steps of a
procedure into the correct sequence (for example DRSABC for a collapsed adult,
or the order for putting on and removing PPE), sorting items into the right
category (person-centred against task-centred practice, signs of dehydration
against signs of poor nutrition, which regulation governs which task), and
matching a term to its meaning (learning disability, autism, reasonable
adjustment and diagnostic overshadowing under standard 16). With the pre-course
check and the final assessment, a learner now makes 85 separate interactions
across the course. The navigation guide describes exactly these activities and
no others.

**3d. Depth and stated learning time.** The course now runs to a timed structure
totalling 1.5 hours, stated identically in the course, the navigation guide and
the submission form:

| Component | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Pre-course knowledge check (5 questions) | 3 |
| Standards 1 to 16: lesson, scenario and check (about 4 minutes each) | 64 |
| Final assessment (32 questions) | 15 |
| Learner feedback and reflection | 3 |
| Observed competency assessment briefing | 5 |
| **Total** | **90** |

Each section provides a substantive refresher against its standard's assessment
outcomes rather than brief explanatory text. In addition, the platform records
each learner's active learning time against the course, so claimed CPD hours
are substantiated by measured engagement, not only by design timings.

**3e. References and further reading.** The course now closes with a references
and further reading section of ten sources, including the Care Certificate
standards (March 2025), the assessor and employer guide, the Code of Conduct,
UK Resuscitation Council guidelines, the Mental Capacity Act Code of Practice,
the national infection prevention and control manual, and the Oliver McGowan
mandatory training programme. These are live links available to the learner
throughout and after the course.

**4a. Title.** "Care Certificate Annual Training" is now used consistently
across all materials, as covered under point 2.

**4b. Description.** The description has been rewritten to reflect the sixteen
standard structure, the annual refresher purpose, the assessment arrangements
and the observed competency requirement.

**4c. Target audience and entry requirements.** The course now states: for
staff who have already completed Care Certificate induction, or hold equivalent
knowledge and experience from their care role. The course is the annual
refresher of that induction, not a replacement for it.

**4d. Duration.** The online course, the navigation guide and the submission
form all now state 1.5 hours, evidenced by the timed structure in 3d. The
previous 35 minute figure related to the earlier six-module version and no
longer appears anywhere.

**5. Observed competency assessment.**

a. Confirmed: the online course is the knowledge component; an observed
competency assessment with the learner's manager completes the requirement.

b. Pass/fail criteria: the observation is conducted against a nine-point
observable criteria checklist (introducing themselves and explaining care
before starting; gaining and recording consent and respecting refusal;
maintaining privacy and dignity throughout; communicating at the person's pace
and checking understanding; following the care plan and reporting changes;
correct hand hygiene at the required moments; recognising the limits of their
role; accurate contemporaneous recording; knowing how to raise a safeguarding
concern). All criteria must be met for a pass. Where any criterion is not met,
the manager records this, the learner receives further support and directed
learning against the specific criteria, and a re-observation is arranged. This
is stated in the navigation guide and the observation record.

c. Verification and quality assurance: the certificate is only issued when both
components are evidenced. Passing the online assessment records the knowledge
component; the certificate remains withheld, and the learner is shown that it
is pending, until their manager records the observed competency sign-off in the
platform. The sign-off captures the assessor's name, date and any notes, and
the issued certificate itself states who verified the observed assessment and
on what date. Managers conducting observations hold relevant experience of the
tasks being observed, in line with the Care Certificate assessor and employer
guide.

**6. End of course assessment.**

a. Attempts are now limited to three. After a third unsuccessful attempt the
assessment locks and the learner is directed back through the learning content;
the assessment reopens once they have re-engaged with the lesson, with the
attempt count reset. This is stated in the navigation guide.

b. The final assessment bank now contains 32 multiple choice questions, two per
standard, and the navigation guide states 32. The earlier 30/20 discrepancy is
resolved by the rebuild.

c. The pre-course knowledge check is now live: five questions taken before the
course begins, graded automatically, with the result shown back to the learner
and compared with their final assessment score as a measure of learning gain.
The navigation guide describes where it appears in the learner journey.

## Advisory recommendations

Although advisory, all three have been actioned in this resubmission:

**Feedback.** Learner feedback is now a required component of course
completion, and is structured against the areas identified: content quality,
ease of navigation, accessibility for all learners, interactivity and
engagement, plus confidence applying the learning and usefulness to role, each
on a five-point scale with free text. The certificate follows completion of the
feedback.

**Accessibility.** The course uses high-contrast text, a consistent layout and
large touch targets, and supports in-course language switching for staff whose
first language is not English. References are provided as downloadable links.
A fuller accessibility review against WCAG guidance is scheduled as part of our
continuous improvement plan.

**Interactivity.** This was the point we took most seriously, and it drove the
rebuild rather than being answered around the edges. Against the two things
specifically asked for:

*Case studies.* Every one of the sixteen standards now has a workplace case
study written for a care setting: a situation the learner recognises, a prompt
asking what they would do, and then modelled best practice to compare their
answer against. They are not illustrations dropped into the text; the learner
commits to a decision before the answer is revealed.

*Links to further reading.* The course closes with ten live, authoritative
sources, available throughout and after the course, and each course now also
displays the relevant Skills for Care guidance for its topic, so further reading
is one tap away rather than a reading list at the end.

Beyond those two, sixteen interactive activities have been added, one per
standard, described in 3c. Taken together the learner meets 85 interactions:
five pre-course questions, sixteen case studies, sixteen activities, sixteen
in-lesson checks and a thirty-two question assessment. A wrong answer at any
point triggers a short targeted re-teach and the question returns on a later
day, so engagement continues after the course is closed.
